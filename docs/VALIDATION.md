# Validation report

**TONIC has been installed and runs on an M-VAVE FM-1.** The checks below were established in software, for every
release: TONIC's engine tests, the host simulator, the browser emulator (the same code in WebAssembly) and fm1-emu,
an instruction-level emulator of the FM-1 that runs the real firmware file. What each environment is and is not: `docs/TEST_ENVIRONMENT.md`.
Status as of 2026-11-09, firmware 0.26 (`release/tonic-0.26.fwsc`, SHA-256 `3cd4fb14…`; 0.1 to 0.25 stay in `release/`; 0.26: 3.6 KB of memory saved, the same sound; 0.25: the crash at every start on some FM-1s fixed; 0.24: the MODWHEEL, AFTERTCH, BREATH and PITCHBND pages gone (the FM-1 has none of them); 0.23: after mono2k, PERFORM's second bank, MIDI out in time with the sound, the overload taking the voice heard least, the text audit; 0.22: the engine screen on EDIT held, Hortator's LFO and GLO pages, one thick frame, every name and value fitting its box; 0.21: the SSAW and ENS engines, BRAIDS without SYNC SIGN SUB, the tabs back, framed values, LFO pictures, the effects resting when idle; ## 2. What is uncertain

| Question | Why it is open | What would settle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real CPU headroom | fm1-emu counts one cycle per instruction, with no cache misses or flash wait states (code runs from XIP flash), so 55 % (0.5.2's DRUM bench; FM 53 %) is a lower bound. The FM-1's clock is 240 or 360 MHz (both readings exist; unmeasured) | the on-device CPU figure on the HOME screen with the bench build (`tools/bench_build.sh`) |
| Audio gap while saving | flash erases stall the CPU; the store waits until the transport is stopped and the sound has died away, but the length of the stall on the real chip is not modelled | saving while a pad is releasing, on the device |
| USB-MIDI, clock in/out, USB-installed update | none of the environments models USB | the device with a DAW |
| Restoring the stock firmware | installing TONIC was done on an FM-1; the way back follows fm1-base's installer and the stock update file | doing it, with a backup made first |
| Panel feel | encoder acceleration and debounce come from fm1-base / felucca-bsp and were exercised only by scripts | playing it |
| Output level and noise | the codec's analog stage is not modelled | listening, measuring the line out |
| How the DRUM kits sound | the 17 models were checked for level, pitch, decay and spectrum only; no one has listened to them, and they were not compared with recordings of the drum machines they follow | listening, on the FM-1's line out |
| Sound vs. a Digitone | no Digitone was available to record; agreement is with the measured OS tables and code (§3), not with recordings | A/B recordings of the same sounds |

## 3. Fidelity to the Digitone

Checked against the OS: the parameter mappings that `docs/RESEARCH.md` §3 marks VERIFIED are implemented from the
measured tables and formulas (as sparse curves, `core/curves.h`; `tools/gen_curves.py` prints each curve's largest error against its table: 0.2–0.5 %
for detune and level, 1–2 % for the operator envelope times) and the amp decay is checked end to end above. Everything marked INFERRED or APPROX there,
the effects in particular, is TONIC's own and has not been compared with the instrument. Two differences are
structural: TONIC runs at 44.1 kHz without the OS's 96 kHz operators (more aliasing at high ratios and index), and
its LP2 and LP4 filters reproduce the OS's constants and measured responses with their own difference equations.
